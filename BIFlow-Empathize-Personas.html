Methodology BIFlow — Empathize Phase

Empathize first, architect second

BIFlow follows the Design Thinking process defined by the Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford (the d.school). The Empathize stage sets assumptions aside to understand needs, frustrations, and goals — before any architecture or feature decision is made.[1][2]

Why Empathy Matters The Problem, In Numbers

Five numbers that define the mission

Pain pointEvidence
Data prep dominates analysts' time~45% of time on data preparation per Anaconda's survey (26%+ on cleaning alone); CrowdFlower found 60% on cleaning, rising to ~80% once collection is included.[5][6]
Poor data quality causes BI failurePoor data quality is estimated to cause 38% of BI project failures.[7]
Business users want self-serviceSelf-service tools are the top-cited driver of BI adoption for 73% of organizations; ~60% of enterprises have implemented self-service BI.[8][7]
Dashboard reliance is rising, trust is fragileDaily-decision dashboard use rose 48% → 67% (2023–2025); yet only ~51% of data leaders trust AI-generated insights, and 40% flag explainability as a key risk.[9][10][11]
Lack of explanation breaks trustInability to explain a decision is the top reason employees override automated output; trustworthy, explainable-AI organizations are ~15× more likely to report strong ROI.[12]

These five points map directly onto BIFlow's mission: automate the pipeline (rows 1–2), keep it self-service (row 3), and keep it trustworthy and explainable (rows 4–5) — exactly why the architecture includes a dedicated BI Auditor / XAI Agent, not just a pipeline of silent transformations.

Persona 01 Sami — the Data / BI Analyst

Sami

Junior–mid BI/Data Analyst inside a business unit (retail, telecom, finance)

Reports to — Analytics Team Lead
Tools today — Excel, SQL, Python/Pandas, Power BI/Tableau
Goal — Deliver a trustworthy, up-to-date dashboard with the right KPIs, fast

I became an analyst to find insights, not to spend my week fixing broken spreadsheets.

Rebuilds profiling and cleaning logic from scratch on every new dataset, and has no easy way to justify why a value was corrected when a stakeholder questions the numbers.

Empathy Map

Says

"Where did this outlier come from?" / "Why are there three date formats in one column?"

Thinks

"If I could just get clean, modeled data automatically, I'd spend my time on the interesting part."

Does

Writes one-off cleaning scripts, manually cross-checks joins, redoes work when new data arrives.

Feels

Frustrated by repetitive low-value work; anxious about silently propagating an error into a KPI.

Pains → Gains — Repetitive manual cleaning, no traceability → wants automated profiling/cleaning with a reviewable log of every transformation.

Persona 02 Sarra — the Business / Department Manager

Sarra

Non-technical manager (Marketing, Operations, Customer Service)

Reports to — Director / VP
Tools today — Whatever dashboard IT/analytics hands her; largely read-only
Goal — Make weekly decisions (budget, staffing, campaigns) on current, understandable KPIs

I don't need more data. I need to know what it means and what to do about it.

Depends entirely on the analytics team's backlog for anything new, and doesn't trust a number she can't explain to her own boss.

Empathy Map

Says

"Can someone just tell me if churn went up or down and why?"

Thinks

"I shouldn't need a data degree to understand my own department's performance."

Does

Requests ad hoc reports, waits days for turnaround, decides on gut feeling when data is late.

Feels

Disempowered when dependent on others; relieved when a dashboard gives a plain-language answer.

Pains → Gains — Slow turnaround, jargon, no autonomy → wants a self-service, auto-updating dashboard with plain-language explanations, not just charts.

Persona 03 Karim — the Executive Decision-Maker

Karim

C-level / senior leadership (CEO, COO, or department VP)

Reports to — Board / shareholders
Tools today — Executive summary decks, occasional dashboard glance
Goal — Make fast, high-stakes strategic calls with confidence

Don't show me the data — show me what it means, and tell me you're sure.

Has been burned before by a "confident-sounding" report resting on bad data; skeptical of AI recommendations without a clear rationale.

Empathy Map

Says

"What's driving this trend, and how confident are we?"

Thinks

"I need to trust this number before I put it in front of the board."

Does

Reviews a handful of top-line KPIs before decisions, asks pointed follow-up questions.

Feels

Under time pressure; wary of being misled by an opaque system.

Pains → Gains — Time scarcity, low tolerance for unexplained numbers → wants concise, high-confidence insights with a visible "why," not a black box.

Persona 04 Youssef — the Data / IT Engineer

Youssef

Data engineer / IT owner responsible for pipeline reliability & governance

Reports to — Head of Data / CTO
Tools today — Custom ETL scripts, cron jobs, manual monitoring
Goal — Keep the pipeline running correctly, auditable, and compliant

I'm fine with automation — as long as I can see exactly what it did and undo it if it's wrong.

Worried about handing decisions to agents he can't fully inspect — accountability still falls on him.

Empathy Map

Says

"Which agent touched this table, and what exactly did it change?"

Thinks

"Automation is only safe if it's traceable and reversible."

Does

Reviews logs after failures, manually patches pipelines, pushes back on "black box" tooling.

Feels

Responsible for outcomes he may not fully control; reassured by clear logs and overrides.

Pains → Gains — Fragile pipelines, no traceability, accountability without visibility → wants a governed pipeline with full lineage and an explicit override/validation layer.

The recurring theme is not "more automation" in isolation — it is automation that remains explainable and controllable. This is precisely why the BI Auditor/XAI Agent is a first-class agent rather than an afterthought, and why the BI Orchestrator Agent exists to coordinate and validate rather than let agents run unchecked.
